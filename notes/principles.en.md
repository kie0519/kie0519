# Question · Know · Prompt · Do

## Question

Desire should first be questioned.

People often describe problems in the shape of answers: build a knowledge base, add an agent, automate the workflow. When an answer arrives too early, the actual need disappears behind it.

Questioning is not asking for more description. It is moving closer to intent: who is facing friction, what change would matter, and what should never be delegated to a machine.

## Know

Deeper questions make judgment clearer.

An AI system needs more than knowledge. It needs a standard for judgment: where data comes from, which boundaries cannot be crossed, what an error costs, and what conditions make an outcome valid.

To know is to establish what “right” means before building.

## Prompt

Once understanding is clear, language must carry it.

The prompt is the visible language; context is the world behind it. Tools, memory, rules, examples, permissions, and history all tell a model where it stands, what it may do, and how it should act.

Prompting is not the search for a magical instruction. It is the translation of human understanding into an environment in which a machine can work.

## Do

After questioning, knowing, and articulating—act.

An elegant generation is not completion. A system must enter a workflow, use tools, absorb feedback, expose failure, and return judgment to a human when necessary.

To do is to let capability produce an outcome, and let the outcome face evaluation.

---

The beginning and the end both point to what we want.

But the first desire is still uncertain. After Question, Know, and Prompt, the final action becomes an informed choice.
